Key Insights

Average rating is almost identical across price segments — Budget 3.02, Mid-Range 3.00 and Premium 3.00 — so paying more does not buy a better-rated product in this catalog.

Top Performers are only 8.1% of products (1,215 products at an average rating of 4.48), but they hold 13.7% of all 75.2M reviews. Low Performers make up 64.1% of the catalog, with an average rating of 2.45.

All 10 products with the highest opportunity scores are daily-use products. Daily-use products average an opportunity score of 3.19, compared with about 2.21 for the other usage frequencies.

Cruelty-free products are 49.4% of the catalog, with no rating or price penalty (3.01 vs 3.00 average rating, $80.23 vs $80.04 average price).

Serum is the largest category (710 products) but its average rating of 2.97 is below the catalog average of 3.00. Contour has the highest average rating (3.13) and Lip Liner the lowest (2.89).

Recommendations

  1. Launch daily-use products first — they take all of the top opportunity scores, so new products should follow that pattern.
  2. Review the Low Performer group (64.1% of the catalog), starting with Lip Liner (2.89) and Eyeliner (2.91), the two lowest-rated categories.
  3. Use cruelty-free as a standard claim in marketing — half the catalog already qualifies, at no cost to rating or price.
  4. For Premium products, show ratings and review counts clearly at the point of sale, because the price on its own does not signal quality.
  5. Work on Serum quality — it is the largest category, so even a small rating improvement there moves more reviews than anywhere else.

Method and Data Notes

Method: the raw data (Cosmetics_products.csv) was validated and cleaned in SQL Server — null and duplicate checks, type casting, and price, rating and review range checks. Price segments, rating segments and the product performance groups were also created in SQL. Exploratory analysis and the Opportunity Score were done in Python (pandas), and the report was built in Power BI Desktop. This page is the same report, with the slicers working the same way.

Data notes: the dataset has 0 missing values and 0 exact duplicate rows across all 15,000 products. It reuses 120 product names across brands and categories (964 name + brand + category repeats), and some listing names do not match their category — for example, a product named “Foundation” listed under Lip Gloss. The data is analysed as supplied. Category average ratings sit in a narrow range (2.89 to 3.13) and price shows no relationship with rating, so the findings focus on the performance groups and review volume rather than small rating differences. All figures come from the project's final Power BI dataset (final_cosmetics_powerbi).
